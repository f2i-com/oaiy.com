The town of Alder Hollow sits in a narrow valley at an elevation of 1,840 metres. It was founded in 1872 by a group of silver miners, but the mine closed in 1911 after the main seam flooded. Today about 2,300 people live there year round, and the population roughly triples every winter when the ski lifts on the northern slope open. The town has one school, two bakeries, a small hospital with twelve beds, and a railway station that receives four trains a day from the city of Brennmoor, which lies 64 kilometres to the south. The river that runs through the valley is called the Tessa. Every August the town holds a festival where residents race handmade wooden boats down the Tessa, and the winning crew is given a copper bell that hangs in the town hall for the following year. The bakery on Mill Street is famous for a rye bread flavoured with caraway and honey, and visitors often buy several loaves before catching the evening train home.
